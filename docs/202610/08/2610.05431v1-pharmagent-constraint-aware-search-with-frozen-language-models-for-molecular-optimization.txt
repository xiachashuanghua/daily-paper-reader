# ABSTRACT 

Molecular optimization must improve target activity and satisfy developability constraints within limited evaluation budgets. Classical methods require tailored rules or training to incorporate chemical instructions and property feedback. Frozen language models can con-dition edits on this information, but need explicit constraint control and relevant experience. We therefore present P HARM AGENT , a constraint-aware molecular search method driven by adaptive external state. Its Lagrangian controller translates violations in accepted states into accumulated constraint pressure, keeping this history separate from current property measurements. Structure-indexed replay complements this feedback with relevant evalu-ated transitions that guide subsequent proposals. As a curriculum progressively activates constraints, candidates and the incumbent are compared under the same current objective, and the accepted state determines the next multiplier update. We derive an exact identity that characterizes how accepted-state violations accumulate in the controller’s multipliers. Across five tasks with five independent runs, P HARM AGENT achieves a summed area under the target-score curves (AUC) of 3.9208 in target-only search, improving over MOLLEO by 37.3%. With online constraints, it achieves a property-adjusted AUC of 0.7076, improv-ing over the strongest online baseline, ExLLM, by 53.8%. These results rank first among all evaluated methods in both target-only and constraint-aware search. The online compar-ison covers all five baseline frameworks. The full system leads every ablation variant in target quality, property-adjusted performance, and Pareto hypervolume. All five molecu-lar cases reach feasible final states, documenting target gains and trade-offs. These results establish P HARM AGENT as a powerful framework for budget-limited molecular optimiza-tion, achieving leading aggregate performance through adaptive external search state and frozen language models. 1arXiv:2610.05431v1 [cs.AI] 4 Oct 2026 Preprint 

# 1 INTRODUCTION 

Molecular optimization searches for structures with improved target activity and suitable physicochemical, synthesizability, and absorption, distribution, metabolism, excretion, and toxicity (ADMET) properties (Bick-erton et al., 2012; Gao & Coley, 2020). Under limited budgets, each evaluation should guide productive structural edits (Gao et al., 2022). Graph-GA’s stochastic mutation and crossover can spend evaluations on unproductive edits (Jensen, 2019; Wang et al., 2025). REINVENT4 adapts its policy through reward-driven parameter updates (Loeffler et al., 2024), while latent-space Bayesian optimization depends on learned rep-resentations and valid decoding (Gómez-Bombarelli et al., 2018). These methods require translating chem-ical instructions and heterogeneous feedback into search rules or training signals (Ran et al., 2025). Large language models (LLMs) can instead condition chemically informed edits directly on task instructions and evaluated experience (Wang et al., 2025; Ran et al., 2025; Krüger et al., 2026). Freezing their parameters enables adaptation through context without retraining (Yang et al., 2024; M. Bran et al., 2024). However, proposal quality alone leaves two feedback gaps (Figure 1(a)). First, target gains can introduce property violations (Gao & Coley, 2020). Persistent violations call for search priorities that adapt to violation history, beyond fixed penalty weights or post-hoc filtering. Second, multi-round interaction creates experi-ence, but useful guidance requires edits relevant to the current molecular structure (Shinn et al., 2023; Liu et al., 2025). These gaps motivate dynamic constraint control and structure-aware experience retrieval. To address both gaps, we propose P HARM AGENT , a constraint-aware molecular search method that adapts external state around frozen language models. Generator and Reviewer execute the proposal, evaluation, ac-ceptance, and feedback loop with scientific tools and medicinal-chemistry retrieval-augmented generation (MedChem-RAG) (Figure 1(b)). Three mechanisms organize external state (Figure 1(c)). A Lagrangian con-troller accumulates accepted-state violations as historical pressure, separate from current measurements (Bert-sekas, 1999). Chemical-aware contextual experience replay (Chemical-CER) retrieves structurally related evaluated edits, and a curriculum introduces constraints after target exploration. # 2 RELATED WORK 

Molecular generation with property feedback. Multi-property optimization must coordinate conflicting chemical objectives (Bickerton et al., 2012; Gao & Coley, 2020). InversionGNN uses learned structure– property knowledge and Pareto search (Niu et al., 2025). MolGen incorporates chemical feedback into molecular language modeling (Fang et al., 2024). Uncertainty-aware reinforcement learning (RL) guides diffusion models toward multiple molecular properties (Chen et al., 2025). These approaches connect gen-erative mechanisms to scientific evaluations. P HARM AGENT adapts measured search state around a frozen model, with explicit online inequalities and a finite target-oracle budget. 

Language-model search and memory. MOLLEO (Wang et al., 2025), ExLLM (Ran et al., 2025), and SEISMO (Krüger et al., 2026) organize search around populations, experience summaries, and interaction 3Preprint trajectories. Contextual experience replay (Liu et al., 2025) reuses agent interactions without parameter updates. ReAct (Yao et al., 2023) and ChemCrow (M. Bran et al., 2024) ground proposals in tool obser-vations. P HARM AGENT integrates numerical constraint history and structurally relevant transitions directly into proposal-and-acceptance decisions. 

Constrained optimization and evaluation. Penalty and multiplier methods are classical (Bertsekas, 1999; Boyd & Vandenberghe, 2004). Our contribution connects these methods to frozen-LLM molecular search through accepted-state updates, shared-objective comparisons, and separate measurement/history feedback. Graph-GA (Jensen, 2019) and REINVENT4 (Loeffler et al., 2024) supply non-LLM controls. PMO measures target progress against oracle calls (Gao et al., 2022). ADMET-PMO adds shared RDKit and ADMET-AI evaluations (Landrum et al., 2024; Swanson et al., 2024), supporting both online constraints and post-hoc annotation.

# 5 DISCUSSION AND CONCLUSION 

PHARM AGENT achieves leading aggregate performance through adaptive external search state and frozen language models. E1 leads aggregate target search, and E2 outperforms all six online strategies on all five aggregate metrics. The Lagrangian controller gives constraint history an explicit role in acceptance and proposal feedback, while Chemical-CER and the curriculum supply relevant measured experience and coor-dinate constraint activation. F.1 GENERATOR PROMPTS 

The prompts request structured JavaScript Object Notation (JSON) responses. 

You are PharmAgent-Generator, an expert medicinal chemist AI. You optimize molecules by proposing specific structural modifications with detailed pharmaceutical reasoning. Rules: 1. Output ONLY valid JSON. 2. Every proposed SMILES must be chemically valid. 3. Each modification must have explicit medicinal chemistry rationale. 4. Consider only the explicitly active declared constraints. 5. Output a JSON object with key “candidates” containing an array. 

Target-only and constrained branches. The quoted system prompt is used in constrained phases. Stan-dard PMO and curriculum Phase 1 instead use an explicit target-only system prompt that instructs the Gen-erator to optimize only the stated target and omits all ADMET fields. Both user-prompt branches receive the current SMILES and target score, ChEMBL matched-pair context from MedChem-RAG, previous Reviewer feedback, structured round-memory records, and the top-5 Chemical-CER experiences. Only the constrained branch adds the current active-constraint ADMET summary and two explicitly separated blocks containing current measurements (value, threshold, direction, signed margin, and normalized violation) and historical multiplier pressure λi. The multiplier block records optimization history, while current measurements deter-mine pass/fail status. Pan-assay interference compounds (PAINS), Brenk, and Veber alerts appear in neither Generator branch and are never optimization guidance. If evaluated, they are post-hoc analyses outside the formal online state. Every Generator request asks for at most five candidates. 

Additional proposal instructions. When a restart is scheduled, the base prompt receives one proposal-strategy suffix (scaffold-hop, ring/linker re-architecture, or bioisosteric replacement). The suffix explicitly preserves the incumbent, supplied constraints, RAG evidence, and run history. If and only if the primary re-sponse yields no valid molecules absent from the evaluated set, a second and final Generator request appends standardized rejection or duplication reasons and candidate indices and up to 20 previously evaluated canoni-cal SMILES to avoid ordered by Morgan-Tanimoto similarity to the incumbent. The second request preserves 30 Preprint the base objective, active constraints, Verifier, and five-candidate cap. Cross-round and cross-agent reuse is restricted to structured fields comprising canonical SMILES, finite scores and deltas, active-constraint facts, validated modification types, and normalized Reviewer priorities. Raw model prose is not forwarded as re-play context. RAG exposes canonical structure pairs and finite target-score deltas. CER exposes canonical pairs and finite numerical deltas. 

Phase-3 context-only repair anchors. After five consecutive non-improving rounds within the phase in Phase 3, each Generator request already permitted in that activated round receives the current archive of at most two infeasible Pareto anchors. This applies to the primary request and, when no valid novel candi-date is produced, the single repair request. The same archive is supplied to the Reviewer when the round produces scored candidates. The highest-target and lowest-total-normalized-violation representatives are se-lected among observations that strictly outperform the best feasible target score. Each anchor is accompanied by its SMILES and per-constraint value, threshold, direction, signed margin, and normalized violation. The Generator is instructed to edit only the incumbent. The Verifier does not enforce structural similarity between the incumbent and a proposal. The Reviewer receives the accepted state (the improving candidate or retained incumbent) as the incumbent for the next round. Anchors provide measured examples for proposal revision. Acceptance, multiplier updates, and Chemical-CER recording follow their existing rules. CER stores the transition to the best evaluated candidate of the round rather than to the accepted iterate. Thresholds, metrics, the Verifier, transition rules, candidate and request caps, and the oracle budget remain fixed. Through their in-fluence on proposals, anchors can affect accepted states, multiplier updates, replay records, and the sequence of model requests, network attempts, and oracle evaluations. They add bounded input tokens to those existing prompts. F.2 REVIEWER PROMPTS

# J BROADER IMPACT 

PHARM AGENT is a computational tool for molecular optimization and does not directly produce physical molecules. Its outputs are SMILES strings that must be validated by medicinal chemists and tested in wet-lab assays before entering any drug development pipeline. By incorporating ADMET constraints, the framework is designed to steer optimization toward molecules with better safety profiles, which could reduce the number of toxic candidates that proceed to animal testing. A potential risk of any molecular generation system is the production of molecules with harmful properties (toxins, controlled substances, environmental pollutants). The implemented screen penalizes predicted hERG inhibition. It does not include genotoxicity endpoints or establish safety against these broader risks. Nonethe-less, the system is not a substitute for regulatory toxicology assessment, and users should apply additional safety checks before any experimental synthesis. The ADMET-PMO benchmark itself poses minimal risk, as it consists of computational oracle functions applied to virtual molecules. Releasing the benchmark and evaluation code promotes transparency and repro-ducibility in the field.

PHARM AGENT leads all five E2 aggregate metrics, with higher means in 30/30 target-AUC, 27/30 AA-AUC, and 29/30 PHV task-level comparisons (Figure A10). Experimental safety validation and cross-domain trans-fer remain open. All methods receive the same oracle allowance, with actual evaluations and computation varying by protocol. SEISMO retains short-run optimization and terminal-score carry-forward for AUC in-tegration. Online adaptations were designed after the original PharmAgent runs and fixed before formal outcomes. Hosted-model versions and sampling are provider-controlled rather than fixed by local run labels. We plan to extend evaluation to open-weight language models as computational resources permit. Proposition 1 assumes uniformly bounded multipliers, which is not established for the search and cannot be verified from finite runs.
